## Introduction

This preface is written to those of you who have chosen to embark on this course. I gather that many of you have chosen to give this a try even though the course may have left you wondering: What the heck is this course actually about? What is expected of me? Or am I in for some relentless, hardcore taxonomy of mind-control techniques, leading naturally into discussion of current politics?

## Genesis of this Course: Getting Perspective on Mind Control

People have struggled throughout history with controlling their own minds as well as other people minds, for just purposes and not so just purposes.
It is comforting to believe, along with Martin Luther King, Jr. that

> *"The arc of the moral universe is long, but it bends toward justice.[^moralarc]"*

[^moralarc]: 
MLK said this in his ["How Long, Not Long" speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Long,_Not_Long).
[The line has evolved from sermons of Theodore Parker since before  the American Civil War](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2012/11/15/arc-of-universe/).


Unfortunately, observing the American and world political scene for the past several decades, I have come to doubt it.
I think of the line from the Paul Simon song "[[American Tune]{.underline}](https://genius.com/Paul-simon-american-tune-lyrics)",

> *“When I think of the road we’re traveling on, I wonder what’s gone wrong. I can’t help it. I wonder what’s gone wrong.”*

Great thinkers and major players in world affairs have spent much effort trying to control  other peoples minds in regards to politics, religion, science, and life in general.
My own particular list of do's and don’ts for how individuals and societies can be constructive, or at least avoid being terribly destructive,
is heavily biased by my own particular history of intense interactions I have had with individuals and by the particular slice of media I have consumed.
This experience leads me to focus on particular notions about mind control and to worry about how other people's minds are being over-controlled by certain myths.
In addtion, my natural inclinations and professional training as a mathematician incline me to think of issues in the world as abstract problem to be solved.

When I started to think of putting together this course, I realized that I needed to get out of my own personal information bubble.
In order to gain a broader perspective and avoid having the course degenerate into narrow commentary on current politics and personal grievances,
or into some sort of rigid ideological framework, I felt the course should be rooted in core facts that all participants have equal access to.
Unfortunately, a consequence of the polarization our society is 
currently experiencing is that we cannot even agree on what facts are important, or even what facts are true.
So, I have decided to step back—or you could call it a retreat—to the world of fiction, where to the core facts are those presented in the four stories  we will explore.


Each story represents a kind of dystopia in which systemic forces are used to destructively control the minds of people in society. 
The protagonists in these stories represent hero figures struggling to stand up to the kind of mind control that society is imposing on them.

A consistent discussion point about all the stories:

> *How are each of the stories relevant to the issues today, as we each see them?*

##  An Arc of Stories and Lenses

Each of these stories alone could be the basis of one or more full courses.
But, I have included all four so that we could see 

I call the four stories we will consider, taken together,  *an* arc of dystopian stories. 





For each story, I will suggest a tool or "lens" we can use to look at the story.
These lenses are just suggestions to foster thinking and discussion; they are not meant to be a barrier of abstractions.
The primary goal will be to encounter the stories in their own terms.


I look forward to hearing your arguments, alternatives, and even complaints.
