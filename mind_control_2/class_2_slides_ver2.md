## *Notes from Underground* (1864): The Outlier **Ignored**

- He warns that reason would make men into piano keys
  - 2 + 2 = 4, forced on us from outside
- He is an outlier society has no room for
  - His schoolmates ignore him or laugh at him
- He watches himself, persecutes himself
  - His "acute consciousness" audits every gesture and anticipates every sneer
- His defiance — spite, perversity, acting against his own interest — is entirely private
- He argues against rational egoism…
  - …but cannot get out of the cave of his own ego
  - He cannot accept love (Liza)

## Stepping Stones: *We* (Outlier **Diagnosed**) → *Brave New World* (Outlier **Removed**)

Fromm calls *We*, *Brave New World* and *1984* a trilogy of "negative Utopias."[^1]

### Yevgeny Zamyatin's *We* (written 1920–21; published in English 1924)

- The One State is a city of glass: the perfectly rational utopia Dostoevsky mocked[^2]
- D-503 is a mathematician and model citizen
- Glass apartments; citizens inform on each other as a civic duty
- He develops a "soul" and discovers that 2 + 2 = 4 is not all there is
- The state treats that as an illness
- The state's final answer: the Great Operation removes imagination

### Aldous Huxley's *Brave New World* (1932)

- Conditioning installs the watcher before birth — little surveillance needed
- A dystopia of pleasure: free sex and the drug soma
- Bernard and Helmholtz push at the cracks and are simply sent to islands
- John the Savage claims "the right to be unhappy" — the Underground Man's argument, reborn

**Fromm's contrast:** *We* and *1984* control through terror plus manipulation; *Brave New World* through mass suggestion, with no need for terror.

## *1984*: From Self-Watching to Being Watched (Disciplinary Surveillance)

- Orwell gives the watcher to the state
  - Telescreens, Thought Police, children who inform on their parents
  - Big Brother: always watching, listening, controlling
  - The Party cannot tolerate a single Underground Man
- Surveillance turns inward (Foucault's Panopticon)[^3]
  - Never knowing when you're watched, you watch yourself
  - Winston polices his face, his sleep-talk, his handwriting
  - By the end he is his own monitor again — now on the Party's behalf
- **Staged shunning:** Goldstein is a manufactured outlier; the Two Minutes Hate is society's rejection performed as a daily ritual
- **The image:** the Underground Man's inner critic was his own — the Party takes it over

## *1984*: From Controlling Behavior to Controlling the Story (Narrative Analysis)

- **Three note-writers, one act of resistance**
  - The Underground Man writes to "gentlemen" who may not exist
  - D-503 writes to unknown readers on other planets
  - Winston writes his diary to the future
  - Each journal is where the self survives — and each state wants it closed
- **Newspeak makes the diary impossible**
  - Shrink the vocabulary until disloyal thoughts can't be formed
  - The Underground Man resists by never stopping talking; Orwell imagines a regime that removes the words
- **The 2 + 2 inversion**
  - Dostoevsky's rebel defies 2 × 2 = 4 — reason itself is the tyrant
  - Orwell's rebel clings to 2 + 2 = 4 — reality itself is under attack

## Bruner's Narrative Terms, Applied to Winston

These are the terms from the Preface reading questions.[^4]

- **Canonical script:** the Party's version of how to think, act, and remember
- **Breach:** the first word Winston writes in the diary
- **Intentional states:** his private thoughts, the one place the telescreen can't see — until Room 101
- **Narrative truth vs. systemic truth:** Winston's memory against the Ministry of Truth and the memory hole
- **Accrual:** can private acts of defiance add up to a movement?
  - Julia, the Brotherhood, Goldstein's book — and why none of it accrues

## Fromm's Afterword: Can a Boot Stamp on a Human Face Forever?

- **Fromm's central question:** can human nature be changed so we forget our longing for freedom, dignity, and love?
- **Severity proves strength:** it takes a brain operation (*We*), drugs and breeding (*Brave New World*), and unlimited torture (*1984*) — which shows how strong the human strivings are
- **The minority of one:** when truth is whatever the Party says, a minority of one must be convinced he is insane — the outlier lens returns
- **Doublethink is not just "them":** Fromm insists *1984* "means us, too"
- **Fromm's hope:** a fully dehumanized world would be a world of madmen, and not a viable one

## The Through-Line: Control Dressed as Care

- The Underground Man has no controller who pretends to love him
- Zamyatin's Benefactor and Huxley's Mustapha Mond promise happiness in exchange for freedom
- Orwell keeps the caring face and drops the promise
  - *Big Brother* is family language
  - O'Brien in Room 101 is tender, almost fatherly — he says he is curing Winston
  - Fromm compares him to Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor — except the Party no longer even pretends to make people happy

## Questions

1. The Underground Man was ignored; Winston is watched every minute. Which is lonelier?
2. Is secret writing to an unknown reader a kind of freedom? What happens when the words themselves are taken away?
3. Can a society force 2 + 2 to equal 4, or 5 — or make it not matter at all?
4. Are you an outlier? Would you rather be ignored, diagnosed, removed, or hunted, broken, and rebuilt?
5. *From Fromm:* do severe methods prove the strength of what they suppress — or even make the defiant stronger?
6. *From the homework:* is history written by the victors? Is truth written by those in power?
