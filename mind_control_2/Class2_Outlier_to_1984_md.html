Notes from Underground (1864): The Outlier Ignored

Human Outlier Analysis

  • No room for him
    • He warns that reason would make men into piano keys
      • 2 + 2 = 4, forced on us from outside
    • He is an outlier society has no room for
      • His schoolmates ignore him or laugh at him
  • Trapped in himself
    • He watches and persecutes himself
      • “Acute consciousness” audits every gesture2
    • His defiance — spite, perversity — is entirely private
    • He argues against rational egoism…
      • …but can’t escape the cave of his own ego
      • He cannot accept love (Liza)

Stepping Stones: Outlier Diagnosed → Outlier Removed

Fromm calls We, Brave New World, and 1984 a trilogy of “negative Utopias.”3

  • Zamyatin’s We (written 1920–21; English 1924)
    • A city of glass: the rational utopia Dostoevsky mocked4
    • D-503: mathematician, model citizen
    • Glass apartments; citizens inform as a civic duty
    • He grows a “soul” — 2 + 2 = 4 isn’t all there is
    • Treated as an illness; the Great Operation removes imagination
  • Huxley’s Brave New World (1932)
    • Conditioning installs the watcher before birth
    • A dystopia of pleasure: free sex and soma
    • Bernard and Helmholtz push at the cracks — sent to islands
    • John the Savage claims “the right to be unhappy” — the Underground Man’s argument, reborn
  • Fromm’s contrast: We and 1984 rule by terror plus manipulation; Brave New World by mass suggestion, with no need for terror.

1984: From Self-Watching to Big Brother Watching

Disciplinary Surveillance

  • Outward
    • Telescreens, Thought Police, children who inform
    • Big Brother: always watching, listening, controlling
    • The Party cannot tolerate a single Underground Man
  • Back inward (the Panopticon)5
    • Never knowing when you’re watched, you watch yourself
    • Winston polices his face, sleep-talk, handwriting
    • By the end, his own monitor — on the Party’s behalf
  • Staged shunning: Goldstein is a manufactured outlier; the Two Minutes Hate is society’s rejection performed as daily ritual.
  • The image: the Underground Man’s inner critic was his own — the Party takes it over.

1984: Newspeak, Controlling the Story

Narrative Analysis

  • Three note-writers
    • The Underground Man writes to “gentlemen” who may not exist
    • D-503 writes to unknown readers on other planets
    • Winston writes his diary to the future
    • Each journal is where the self survives — and each state wants it closed
  • Newspeak makes the diary impossible
    • Shrink the vocabulary until disloyal thoughts can’t be formed
    • The Underground Man resists by never stopping talking; Orwell imagines a regime that removes the words
  • The 2 + 2 inversion6
    • Dostoevsky: defy 2 × 2 = 4 — reason is the tyrant
    • Orwell: cling to 2 + 2 = 4 — reality is under attack

Bruner’s Terms, Applied to Winston

Narrative Analysis — terms from the Preface reading questions7

  • Canonical script: the Party’s version of how to think, act, and remember
  • Breach: the first word Winston writes in the diary
  • Intentional states: his private thoughts — the one place the telescreen can’t see, until Room 101
  • Narrative vs. systemic truth: Winston’s memory against the Ministry of Truth and the memory hole
  • Accrual: can private defiance add up to a movement? Julia, the Brotherhood, Goldstein’s book — and why none of it accrues

Fromm’s Afterword: Can a Boot Stamp on a Human Face Forever?

  • Central question: can human nature be changed so we forget our longing for freedom, dignity, and love?
  • Severity proves strength: a brain operation (We), drugs and breeding (Brave New World), unlimited torture (1984) — the cure shows how strong the striving is
  • The minority of one: when truth is whatever the Party says, a minority of one must believe he is insane — the outlier lens returns
  • Doublethink is not just “them”: Fromm insists 1984 “means us, too”
  • Fromm’s hope: a fully dehumanized world would be a world of madmen — not a viable one

Above quote from ending of Fromm’s Afterward:

I am sure that neither Orwell nor Huxley or Zamyatin wanted to insist that this world of insanity is bound to come. On the contrary, it was quite obviously their intention to sound a warning by showing where we are headed for unless we succeed in a renaissance of the spirit of humanism and dignity which is at the very roots of Occidental culture. Orwell, as well as the two other authors, is simply implying that the new form of managerial industrialism, in which man builds machines which act like men and develops men who act like machines, is conducive to an era of dehumanization and complete alienation, in which men are transformed into things and become appendices to the process of production and consumption . All three authors imply that this danger exists not only in Communism of the Russian or Chinese versions, but that it is a danger inherent in the modem mode of production and organization, and relatively independent of the various ideologies. Orwell, like the authors of the other negative Utopias, is not a prophet of disaster. He wants to warn and to awaken us. He still hopes – but in contrast to the writers of the Utopias in the earlier phases of Western society, his hope is a desperate one. The hope can be realized only by recognizing, so 1984 teaches us, the danger with which all men are confronted today, the danger of a society of automatons who will have lost every trace of individuality, of love, of critical thought, and yet who will not be aware of it because of “doublethink”. Books like Orwell’s are powerful warnings, and it would be most unfortunate if the reader smugly interpreted 1984 as another description of Stalinist barbarism, and if he does not see that it means us, too.

The Through-Line: Control Dressed as Care

  • No controller: the Underground Man has no one who pretends to love him
  • Happiness for freedom: Zamyatin’s Benefactor and Huxley’s Mustapha Mond make the trade
  • Caring face, no promise: “Big Brother” is family language; O’Brien, tender in Room 101, says he is curing Winston
  • Fromm: O’Brien is Dostoevsky’s Grand Inquisitor — except the Party no longer even pretends to make people happy

Questions for the Room

  1. The Underground Man was ignored; Winston is watched every minute. Which is lonelier?
  2. Is secret writing to an unknown reader a kind of freedom? What happens when the words are taken away?
  3. Can a society force 2 + 2 to equal 4, or 5 — or make it not matter at all?
  4. Are you an outlier? Would you rather be ignored, diagnosed, removed, or hunted, broken, and rebuilt?
  5. From Fromm: do severe methods prove the strength of what they suppress — or make the defiant stronger?
  6. From the homework: is history written by the victors? Is truth written by those in power?

What Comes Next: The Matrix

  • Blue pill: unknowingly accept a virtual reality
  • Red pill: see the truth — even 2 + 2 = 4 is just code underneath: 10 + 10 = 100 in binary
  • The controller changes again: the Architect doesn’t watch you or break you — he builds the world you live in.
