## Slide 1 — From Outlier to Watched

- Three lenses, one story: *where does the watcher live, and who owns the story?*
  - **Human Outlier Analysis** — *Notes from Underground*
  - **Disciplinary Surveillance** — *1984* (Big Brother is watching)
  - **Narrative Analysis** — *1984* (Newspeak, the Ministry of Truth)
- Two stepping stones in between: Zamyatin's *We* (1921) and Huxley's *Brave New World* (1932)

## Slide 2 — The Starting Point: An Outlier Nobody Bothers to Control

- Society doesn't persecute the Underground Man — it ignores him
  - The officer who moves him aside like furniture; the schoolmates who forgot him; Zverkov's dinner
  - His rebellion — spite, perversity, acting against his own interest — is entirely private
- But there *is* a watcher: the Underground Man himself
  - His "acute consciousness" audits every gesture and anticipates every sneer
  - The most surveilled man in literature — and he does it to himself[^1]

## Slide 3 — Stepping Stones: How the Outlier's Fate Changes

- **Zamyatin's *We*: the outlier is diagnosed**
  - D-503, the model citizen, develops a "soul" — treated as an illness
  - Glass apartments; citizens inform on each other as a civic duty
  - The state's final answer: the Great Operation removes imagination
- **Huxley's *Brave New World*: the outlier is gently removed**
  - Bernard and Helmholtz are simply sent to islands
  - Conditioning installs the watcher before birth — little surveillance needed
  - John the Savage claims "the right to be unhappy" — the Underground Man's argument, reborn
- **The thread:** ignored → diagnosed → removed → hunted, broken, and rebuilt (Orwell)

## Slide 4 — Hinge 1: From Self-Watching to Being Watched

- **Outward:** Orwell gives the watcher to the state
  - Telescreens, Thought Police, children who inform on their parents
  - The Party cannot tolerate a single Underground Man
  - Zamyatin built the house of glass; Orwell added a watcher that also listens
- **Back inward:** never knowing when you're watched, you watch yourself
  - Winston polices his face, his sleep-talk, his handwriting
  - By the end, he is his own monitor again — now on the Party's behalf
- **Staged shunning:** Goldstein is a manufactured outlier; the Two Minutes Hate is society's rejection performed as daily ritual
- **The image:** the Underground Man's inner critic was his own — the Party colonizes it

## Slide 5 — Hinge 2: From Controlling Behavior to Controlling the Story

- **Three note-writers, one act of resistance**
  - The Underground Man writes to "gentlemen" who may not exist
  - D-503 writes to unknown readers on other planets
  - Winston writes his diary to the future
  - Each journal is where the self survives — and each state wants it closed
- **Newspeak makes the diary impossible**
  - Shrink the vocabulary until disloyal thoughts can't be formed
  - The Underground Man resists by never stopping talking; Orwell imagines a regime that removes the words
- **The 2 + 2 inversion**
  - Dostoevsky's rebel defies 2 × 2 = 4 — reason itself is the tyrant
  - Orwell's rebel clings to 2 + 2 = 4 — reality itself is under attack

## Slide 6 — The Through-Line: Control Dressed as Care

- The Underground Man has no controller who pretends to love him
- Zamyatin's *Benefactor* and Huxley's Mustapha Mond promise happiness in exchange for freedom
- Orwell keeps the caring face and drops the promise
  - *Big Brother* is family language
  - O'Brien in Room 101 is tender, almost fatherly — he says he is curing Winston
- Sets up what comes next: the Architect (*The Matrix*) and Tilly (*The Perfect Match*)

## Slide 7 — Questions for the Room

1. The Underground Man was ignored; Winston is watched every minute. Which is lonelier?
2. Both men write secret notes to a reader who may never exist. Why does writing feel like freedom — and what happens when the words themselves are taken away?
3. Dostoevsky's rebel says 2 + 2 = 5; Orwell's insists it's 4. Who has it right — and does the answer depend on who's in charge?
4. Huxley exiles outliers to comfortable islands; Orwell breaks them in Room 101. If you were the outlier, which would you choose — and which leaves you more yourself?
