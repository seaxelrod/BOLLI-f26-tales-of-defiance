  • [stated] The fictional student archetypes (Dorothy, Marvin, Fran, Arthur) are four competing answers to the course’s central question, not a hierarchy
  • [stated] Controllers across all four works share a key rhetorical move: reframing control as care — a through-line worth foregrounding
  • [stated] The darker voice exercises may benefit from counter-voices to balance the room
  • [stated] Students are intellectually accepting of uncertainty but emotionally unresolved, and prone to connecting course material to current U.S. political events — facilitating that connection productively is a design challenge
  • [stated] Scott works iteratively: each session builds on prior output, from abstract to embodied, from questions to voices to full documents
  • [stated] Prefers concrete recommendations over open menus
  • [stated] Course design integrates theory, fiction, and simulated student voice as parallel tracks
