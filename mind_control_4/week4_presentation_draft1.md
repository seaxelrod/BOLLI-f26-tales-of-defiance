# The Story Through Our Lenses

## What Does the System Do with a Rebel?

- \Cteal{The Underground Man is ignored.}
- \Cteal{Winston is broken and rebuilt.}
- \Cteal{Neo is hunted, to be deleted.} \vspace*{0.2in}

- \Cred{Sai is offered a job.} \vspace*{0.3in}

- Each step is gentler. Is each one harder to resist?

:::notes
- Rinn: "I want you to come and work for Centillion."
- His reason: people who can see Tilly's flaws are the best ones to fix them. The system turns its critics into improvements.
- This is the academic point of the False Needs lens: a comfortable society absorbs its opposition instead of crushing it (see the Extra Slide on Marcuse).
- The story never says whether Sai and Jenny take the job. Jenny is still around at the end, and she still refuses to "share."
:::

## Wants Made for Us

- A smoothie instead of the coffee he wanted. \vspace*{0.15in}
- A sudden craving for Japanese dessert, right on cue. \vspace*{0.15in}
- A "regimen for consumption of leisure." \vspace*{0.3in}

- \Cpurple{Did Tilly figure out what he wanted,}
- \Cpurple{or put the thought into his head?}

:::notes
- Jenny asks Sai: "Do you even know what you really want any more?" Sai repeats it to himself during the date.
- Sai's best defense: advertising simply matches desire with satisfaction. "The perfect product finds the perfect consumer, the perfect girl finds the perfect boy." It deserves a serious hearing.
- The date with Ellen goes perfectly, and it is boring: "There were no surprises, no thrill of finding the truly new."
- When Sai saves money, Tilly calls it over-saving. His savings rate is "on target." She has plotted how much he should spend each day.
- Academic name: False Needs (Marcuse).
:::

## Watched for Profit

- "Gaps in sharing can cause your profile to be incomplete." \vspace*{0.15in}
- The off switch has a hidden override. \vspace*{0.15in}

- \Cred{"If it can't be found by Centillion, it doesn't exist."}

## Wants Made for Us: False Needs (Herbert Marcuse)

- *One-Dimensional Man* (1964) \vspace*{0.15in}
- A society can create wants in us, satisfy them, and leave us sure they were ours all along. \vspace*{0.15in}
- True needs (food, shelter, freedom) versus needs imposed for someone else's benefit. \vspace*{0.15in}
- A comfortable society doesn't crush dissent; it absorbs it.

:::notes
- Rinn hiring Sai and Jenny is the absorption point in action.
- Background: Kellner, "From 1984 to One-Dimensional Man" (in the project files).
- Further reading, from the references page: Abadir, "You've Been Hacked: A Marcusian Analysis of Addictive Technologies" (short); Şimşek, "One-Dimensional Algorithm" (heavy). Şimşek argues Marcuse underrated how people push back, which is worth weighing against Jenny.

**Decision points**

## Watched for Profit: Surveillance Capitalism (Shoshana Zuboff)

- *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (2019) \vspace*{0.15in}
- Companies profit by watching what we do, predicting what we'll do next, and nudging us toward it. \vspace*{0.15in}
- What gets sold is the prediction, and the nudge.

:::notes
- In the story: Tilly's suggestions are paid placements. Jenny: "You mean some advertiser paid Centillion to pitch it at you."
- Ken Liu, in the Lightspeed author spotlight, worries less about the machines than about "those who hold the keys to the databases."

## Rinn's Full Argument

- There is no such thing as neutral information. \vspace*{0.1in}
- To search for what matters to you, we must know all about you. \vspace*{0.1in}
- That is "indistinguishable from filtering, from manipulation." \vspace*{0.1in}
- We are cyborgs; our electronic copies are us. \vspace*{0.1in}
- Destroy Centillion, and something worse takes its place.

:::notes

:::
