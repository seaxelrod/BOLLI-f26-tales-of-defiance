# Extra Slides

## False Needs (Herbert Marcuse)

- \Cteal{A society can create wants in us, satisfy them,}
- \Cteal{and leave us sure they were ours all along.} \vspace*{0.2in}

- In the story:
   - Sai "chooses" a smoothie, a dessert, a girlfriend.
   - Rebels are hired, not jailed.

:::notes
- Source: *One-Dimensional Man* (1964).
- Marcuse separates true needs (food, shelter, freedom) from needs imposed for someone else's benefit.
- A comfortable society doesn't crush dissent; it absorbs it. Rinn hiring Sai and Jenny is the absorption point in action.
- Background: Kellner, "From 1984 to One-Dimensional Man" (in the project files).
- Further reading on the references page: Abadir, "You've Been Hacked" (short); Şimşek, "One-Dimensional Algorithm" (heavy). Şimşek argues Marcuse underrated how people push back, which is worth weighing against Jenny.

**Decision points**

:::

## Surveillance Capitalism (Shoshana Zuboff)

- \Cteal{Companies profit by watching what we do,}
- \Cteal{predicting what we'll do next, and nudging us toward it.} \vspace*{0.2in}

- \Cred{Behavioral surplus:} data beyond what a service needs, used to predict and steer us. \vspace*{0.2in}

- In the story:
   - Tilly's suggestions are paid pitches.
   - [*"There's a gap of about twelve hours during which I couldn't acquire data about you."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-so-what)

:::notes
- Source: *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (2019).
- Tilly goes on: "To prevent degradation in my ability to help you, I recommend that you fill me in." The surplus is the point; the help is the bait.
- Video: [Behavioral surplus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KKv2ZDbMyb4&t=674s) (11:14), from [Shoshana Zuboff's The Age of Surveillance Capitalism: A Guide to Fighting Back](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KKv2ZDbMyb4), Epoch Philosophy.
- The video's "third modernity" point: companies present themselves as friendly and cooperative while quietly extracting our data ([6:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KKv2ZDbMyb4&t=370s)). That is "Make things better!" in a sentence.
- What gets sold is the prediction, and the nudge.
- Ken Liu, in the Lightspeed author spotlight, worries less about the machines than about "those who hold the keys to the databases."
- The Zuboff op-ed assigned in the Matrix week fits here.

## How Prescient Was Ken Liu?

- \Cteal{Already in the news when he wrote (2012):}
   - Street View cars recording home Wi-Fi traffic (2010)
   - Google bypassing Safari's privacy settings (fined 2012) \vspace*{0.3in}

- What did he see coming?

:::notes
- Liu doesn't present these as predictions. In the story they are old news ([Jenny's documents](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-sniffing-wifi)):
   - The cars: Google first said its Street View cars logged only network names and router IDs. In May 2010 it admitted they had also captured "payload data" (bits of emails and web pages, even passwords) from open networks, and called it a mistake. Regulators in many countries investigated.
   - The security settings: in 2012 Google paid the FTC a record $22.5 million for getting around Safari's default block on tracking cookies.
- So the story's surveillance tools were real. What Liu saw ahead was the assistant that is always talking and always there, and a company that becomes the filter for all news.
- Parallels: "arrange the world's information to ennoble the human race" vs. Google's "organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful"; "I Trust You" vs. "I'm Feeling Lucky"; "Make things better!" vs. "Don't be evil"; ShareAll vs. Facebook.
- Phones reading keystrokes from motion-sensor vibrations had been shown by university researchers around 2011.
:::
