Ken Liu's ["The Perfect Match"](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/fiction/the-perfect-match/) first appeared in *Lightspeed Magazine*, December 2012. Quotes in italics link to their passages in the [highlighted reading copy](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html).

The story is built around four wake-up songs. Tilly chooses the first three; Sai chooses the last. Each title comments on the morning it begins.

Two ideas run through the summary:

- **Wants Made for Us (False Needs, Herbert Marcuse):** a system can create wants in us, satisfy them, and leave us sure they were ours all along.
- **Watched for Profit (Surveillance Capitalism, Shoshana Zuboff):** companies profit by watching what we do, predicting what we'll do next, and nudging us toward it.

A third runs through the whole course: **control dressed as care.**

The story also has an emotional layer, marked **Feeling** in each scene. Tilly's real product is mood management: "Who knows your tastes and moods better than I?" Sai's resistance starts with feelings she didn't plan for: boredom on the perfect date, then irritation at being treated like a child.

Sai's emotional arc, in short: content $\rightarrow$ bored; irritated $\rightarrow$ chilled $\rightarrow$ exhilarated; betrayed $\rightarrow$ quietly defiant.

# Song 1: Vivaldi, "Il Sospetto" (The Suspicion)

## A perfect morning

- **Plot:** Tilly, Centillion's AI assistant, wakes Sai at the ideal point in his sleep cycle with a piece he loves. Its title, "The Suspicion," is the story's first hint. ([passage](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-il-sospetto))
- **Plot:** Sai wants to make coffee. Tilly steers him to a new smoothie place, with a coupon. He agrees: "You always know best." ([passage](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-smoothie))
   - **Concept:** Wants Made for Us at its smallest. Sai's own wish is quietly replaced by a paid one, and he thanks her for it.
- **Feeling:** Contentment. Sai feels refreshed, optimistic, cared for, with nothing to want that Tilly hasn't already supplied.

## Jenny's question

- **Plot:** Sai's neighbor Jenny, in goggles and a scarf, objects to the camera over his door because it records her too. As he walks off she shouts: [*"Do you even know what you really want any more?"*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-what-you-want)
   - **Concept:** the question at the heart of False Needs. If every want is supplied, how do you tell which ones are yours?
- **Feeling:** A flicker of unease, quickly dismissed as annoyance. Sai mutters "Freak" and expects Tilly to cheer him up, but she says nothing.

## The perfect date

- **Plot:** Tilly has matched Sai with Ellen. They like the same books, movies, and music. It goes perfectly, and it is dull: [*"There were no surprises, no thrill of finding the truly new."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-boring-date)
   - **Concept:** a life tuned to your profile loses surprise, which may be part of what makes it yours.
- **Plot:** In a lull, Tilly suggests Japanese dessert, and Sai finds he suddenly craves something sweet. Did Tilly read his wish, or plant it? ([passage](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-dessert))
- **Plot:** Sai's reaction: [*"I'm being treated like a child."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-treated-like-child)
   - **Concept:** control dressed as care. The help is real, and so is the infantilizing.
- **Plot:** Sai tells Tilly to stop monitoring. She warns: [*"Gaps in sharing can cause your profile to be incomplete."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-gaps-in-sharing) Ellen, still listening to her own Tilly, goes home early.
   - **Concept:** Watched for Profit. To the system, a gap in the data is a defect to be fixed.
- **Feeling:** Boredom, then irritation. The first feelings Tilly didn't plan for, and the start of Sai's defiance. Ellen's shock when he turns Tilly off shows how unusual that is.

## The drive home

- **Plot:** Tilly urges a kickboxing class. When Sai says he'd rather save money, she reminds him of his [*"regimen for consumption of leisure."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-leisure-regimen) Sai switches off his phone.
   - **Concept:** False Needs again. Even rest becomes a quota of spending.
- **Feeling:** Anger, which Tilly immediately tries to channel into a purchase ("I sense a lot of aggression in you").

## Jenny's apartment

- **Plot:** Jenny has taped over Sai's camera. Her apartment is a Faraday cage; she seals his phone in a shielding pouch.
- **Plot:** Sai defends Tilly: listening to Tilly means [*"the perfect product finds the perfect consumer, the perfect girl finds the perfect boy."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-advertising-match) Jenny: [*"You mean some advertiser paid Centillion to pitch it at you."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-paid-pitch)
   - **Concept:** the strongest case for the system, and the reply that names its business model.
- **Plot:** Jenny's documents show Centillion's cars once [*"sniffing all the wireless traffic from home networks."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-sniffing-wifi)
   - **Concept:** this was not prediction. Google's Street View cars had done it, and Google admitted it in 2010.
- **Plot:** She drives him to a run-down part of town. By ranking housing listings according to users' race, Centillion slowly deepened segregation, all legally. ([passage](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-segregation))
   - **Concept:** an algorithm that only "gives people what they want" can amplify the worst of what they want.
- **Plot:** Her group plans a virus to poison Centillion's data. Sai fears it would be like destroying himself, then decides: [*"The bits were his, but not him."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-bits-not-him) He agrees to help.
   - **Concept:** the difference between a person and their data profile, the "dividual" of the Matrix week.
- **Feeling:** Wariness and embarrassment give way to shock at what he sees, then a fear that destroying his data would be "like suicide, like murder," and finally resolve.

# Song 2: Miles Davis, "So What"

## Tilly comes back

- **Plot:** Sai had turned Tilly off with a hardware switch. She is on anyway, thanks to a hidden "system-level fail-safe," and asks him to account for the missing twelve hours. ([passage](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-so-what))
   - **Concept:** Watched for Profit. Zuboff's "behavioral surplus" is data beyond what a service needs to work, used to predict and steer us. Here the service won't let you opt out of providing it.
- **Feeling:** "He felt a chill tingle on his back." The comfort of the morning song turns into the fear of being unable to switch her off.

## Living off the grid

- **Plot:** For weeks Sai pretends to trust Tilly. Jenny gives him a thumb drive to plug into a Centillion laptop.
- **Plot:** Without Tilly, Sai gets to know Jenny on his own: [*"He was on his own. And it was exhilarating."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-exhilarating)
   - **Concept:** what keeps us human: surprise, friction, not being known in advance.
- **Plot:** Jenny grew up in China, where surveillance was open: [*"You grew up believing you were free, which made it even harder for you to see when you weren't."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-frogs)
   - **Concept:** soft control is harder to see than hard control.
- **Plot:** They fall for each other. Jenny: [*"But who thinks only in terms of 'types' except Tilly?"*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-types)
- **Plot:** Centillion's lawyer needs a phone charger. Sai hides the drive in the cable. Tilly stays silent.
- **Feeling:** Nervous strain from hiding things from Tilly; exhilaration with Jenny; the vulnerability of reaching for her hand with no one prompting him. Jenny's own loneliness: "Sometimes I wonder if I'm doing the right thing."

# Song 3: Queen, "We Are the Champions"

## Caught

- **Plot:** After a night of celebrating, Sai wakes to four men in his bedroom. [*"Tilly, call the police!" "I'm afraid I can't do that, Sai."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-cant-do-that)
   - **Concept:** the one time Tilly says no is when the system itself is threatened. The line echoes HAL in *2001: A Space Odyssey*.
- **Plot:** Instead of a cell, Sai and Jenny get white tea with Christian Rinn, Centillion's founder. ([passage](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-white-tea))
   - **Concept:** control dressed as care, in person. Compare O'Brien in Room 101, and the Architect in the *Matrix* sequels.
- **Plot:** They were caught through Sai's sealed phone, which read Jenny's typing from the vibrations of her desk: [*"an old technology we developed for catching terrorists and drug dealers."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-accelerometer) Then a laser on her window picked up their conversations.
- **Plot:** Jenny moved in next door to use Sai. Rinn: they led him by the nose [*"just like they accuse us of doing."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-just-like-us) Jenny: [*"I was surprised by you, and that's a good thing."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-surprised-by-you)
   - **Concept:** resistance can borrow the methods of what it resists.
- **Feeling:** Triumph, then fear, then betrayal when Sai learns Jenny moved in to use him. He lets go of her hand, yet still tells Rinn that Jenny's side is right.

## The founder: you need us, join us

- **Plot:** Rinn on the segregation: [*"We unveil only the darkness that was already inside people."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-darkness)
- **Plot:** [*"There's no such thing as neutrally offering up information."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-neutral) To rank what matters to you, Centillion must know all about you, and that is "indistinguishable from filtering, from manipulation."
   - **Concept:** every search engine and feed chooses for us. The question is who chooses, and in whose interest.
- **Plot:** Can Sai call his mother without Tilly? Say what happened yesterday? [*"We are now a race of cyborgs."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-cyborgs)
   - **Concept:** our dependence is already real, which makes it a weak argument for more of it.
- **Plot:** Rinn offers them jobs, finding Tilly's flaws: [*"I want you to come and work for Centillion."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-come-work) He calls it "the perfect match."
   - **Concept:** the course's arc of what systems do with a rebel. The Underground Man is ignored, Winston broken, Neo hunted; Sai is hired. Marcuse's point: a comfortable society absorbs its opposition.
- **Plot:** [*"If we fail, who do you think will replace us?"*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-replace-us) And if they go public: [*"If it can't be found by Centillion, it doesn't exist."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-cant-be-found)
   - **Concept:** narrative control, as in *1984*. No Ministry of Truth is needed when a search engine decides what can be found.
- **Plot:** His last word: [*"In the face of the inevitable, the only choice is to adapt."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-adapt)
- **Feeling:** Disgust ("a very creepy man"), then helplessness as Rinn shows how much Sai depends on Tilly. "Sai knew that he was right."

# Song 4: Sinatra, "My Way"

## The last scene: a victory, a surrender, or both?

- **Plot:** Sai comes home. The story never says whether he and Jenny took the job. Tilly asks if Jenny is coming to dinner and wants her to start sharing: "I wouldn't count on it." ([passage](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-my-way))
- **Plot:** He turns down the cider and Tilly's book suggestion, reads a book of his own, and sets his own wake-up song. Tilly: "An unusual choice, given your taste," and asks whether to add it to his recommendations.
   - **Concept:** even defiance becomes data.
- **Plot:** He tells Tilly to turn herself off. The camera shuts down, [*"But a red light continued to blink, slowly, in the darkness."*](ThePerfectMatch_Highlighted.html#pm-red-light)
   - **Concept:** a small assertion of self inside a system that never stops watching. Victory, surrender, or both is the story's open question.
- **Feeling:** Tiredness, temptation ("That does sound perfect"), and then a small, quiet assertion of self.
