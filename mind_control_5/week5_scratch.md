# Comments on the video for Week 5

- **Heffner brings up Dostoevsky's Grand Inquisitor (19:07).** That fits your arc well: the course started with Dostoevsky, and it ends with him in its last homework. The Inquisitor's case is that people want bread, miracles and authority more than freedom, which is control presented as care once again. Asimov answers with the Constitutional Convention rather than a hero. That could be a good prompt on Monday.
- **Asimov's 2 + 2 line (5:29)** comes with an idea your students will recognize. Con men "of the spirit" sell security and certainty that don't exist, and the buyers pay gladly. That's close to False Needs and to what Tilly sells.
- **Science fiction writers as "scouts" (10:27).** Their job is to report "that way lies quicksand" more than to predict. That maps neatly onto "did the lenses help?"
- **"The grimness is true, the happy ending is false" (12:46),** next to his closing optimism that nations cooperate because "the other way there is death." That pairing could prompt a question about whether his optimism is itself a happy ending.
- **"Drops… to that small pond" (0:58)** echoes your own "small-pond thought-leader" line, if you ever want to use it.
- **The date is worth telling students.** It's 1988: Reagan and Gorbachev, the ozone layer, acid rain. Some of Asimov's examples have aged and some haven't, which feeds your "where do we go from here?" question.
