## Executive summary of Power Point Presentatoin

- This is Session 1 of Scott Axelrod's course on defiance and mind control. It uses Dostoevsky's *Notes from Underground* (1864) as the first tale. The deck places the book in 1860s Russia, as a rebuttal to Chernyshevsky's rationalist "Crystal Palace" utopia (slides 3–7).
- The Underground Man argues that people will act against their own interest to prove they are free ("not piano keys"). He also suspects that people would beg to be managed again if given more independence. The session presents this through two Larry Cedar clips and a three-part Ruth and Dev dialogue (slides 8–12).
- The deck closes with homework questions and a "human outlier" lens drawn from Foucault (slides 13–14). It then links the book to *1984* (the next tale) and to the course's dystopian arc and critical lenses (slides 15–18).

# Prompt: Can you make suggestions?

## Response

Your instinct fits the deck. It front-loads about 12 slides of content before the questions, so the main risk is running out of time before the discussion starts. I don't know your session length, so this assumes about 90 minutes. Scale the numbers if it's longer.

**Suggested time budget**

| Segment | Slides | Time | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Welcome and roadmap | 1–2 | 3 min | Brief |
| Context | 3–7 | 12 min | Light lecture, with check-ins |
| Clip 1 and dialogue | 8–11 | 15 min | Participatory |
| Clip 2 | 12 | 3 min | Watch and react |
| **Questions (the main part)** | 13–14 | **35 min** | Discussion |
| Segue to Tale 2 | 15–18 | 10 min | Short, then preview |
| Buffer | | 12 min | Overflow from discussion |

That puts roughly 45% of the time on discussion and gives the buffer to the part most likely to run long.

**How much to gloss over, and where to invite questions**

- **Slide 3 (timeline):** Don't walk through every date. Point out three anchors: the 1861 emancipation, the 1863–64 exchange between Chernyshevsky and Dostoevsky, and the 1881 assassination. Then say "this is here for reference."
- **Slides 4–5 (setting and the Crystal Palace):** Spend about 5 minutes total. Hold questions to a single check-in: "Does anyone want to say anything about rational egoism before we go on?" Students will want to talk about this, so bank that interest for the discussion segment.
- **Slide 6 (two Siberias):** This is the quickest one to gloss. Say "punished first versus writes first, and it matters because it shapes who each man is arguing with," and move on.
- **Slide 7 (the Underground Man):** Slow down here. It sets up the central ideas the questions rely on: "not piano keys," 2×2=4, and the anthill. Ask one question, such as "Is wanting to be free worth acting against your own interest?"
- **Clips and dialogue (8–12):** The Ruth and Dev dialogue works as an interactive piece in its own right. Pause after Part 2 and ask "Is he a hero of freedom, or just spiteful?" Pause again after Part 3 and ask "Would we notice we'd stopped choosing?" Those pauses preview your discussion questions.
- **Slides 15–18 (segue):** Treat this as a trailer. Spend about 2 minutes on the legacy slide, then link 2×2=4 to *1984* and stop. The lenses on slide 18 can be a "coming attractions" slide, since they are covered in the Preface.

**Making the questions section work**

- **Don't read the questions aloud in order.** Slide 13 has four concrete questions (situation, how he got there, dilemma, personality). They are easy, so have students answer in 5 minutes by pair-share or a quick popcorn round. That builds confidence before the harder questions.
- **Spend most of the time on slide 14.** The questions on sympathy, dysfunction versus insight, and what he sees that others don't can each fill 8–10 minutes. Choose two or three rather than covering all four.
- **Use the human outlier question as your anchor.** "Do we lose something if we dismiss him as mad?" connects Foucault to the Underground Man. It is also the thread that continues into *1984*.
- **Seed one provocative question.** For example: "Do you ever want to be managed for your own good?" It ties to the closing line of the clip and to the whole course. BOLLI students tend to respond well to personal, real-world connections.
- **Use the room's experience.** Ask "Has anyone felt like the outlier in a group?" before the abstract questions.

**Practical tips for BOLLI-style classes**

- **Hold back your own opinion** until at least two or three students have spoken. Prepare a follow-up, such as "Say more about that" or "Who sees it differently?"
- **Keep the pre-read light.** Since students did the homework, begin with "What stayed with you?" You'll find out what they remember and what interests them.
- **Add a "parking lot".** Note questions that come up during the context section and promise to return to them in the discussion. It prevents tangents without shutting people down.
- **Have a plan for dominant voices.** Pair-share first, or ask "Let's hear from someone who hasn't spoken yet."
- **Use the speaker notes.** Add a one-line "discussion prompt" to each context slide so you remember where to pause.
