# II: Information related to "The Perfect Match"


## References related to **False Needs** (Herbert Marcuse): a system can create wants in us, satisfy them, and leave us sure they were ours all along.

- ["Are We Offloading Too Much of Our Thinking to AI?," Art Fish Intelligence](https://www.artfish.ai/p/offloading-thinking-to-ai), Yennie Jun, July 2026
   - The easiest read here. Jun, who works on Google's Gemini, uses the story to ask how much of our thinking we hand to AI. When an assistant shapes our preferences, we lose track of which ones we chose.

- ["Algorithmic Love: Should Zeroes and Ones Pick 'The One?'," The Politic](https://thepolitic.org/algorithmic-love-should-zeroes-and-ones-pick-the-one/), Yale student magazine
   - A student writer compares Tilly with a real campus matchmaking app. Do we want an algorithm to choose our partners, or were we taught to want that?

- ["You've Been Hacked: A Marcusian Analysis of Addictive Technologies," LSE Undergraduate Political Review](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/lseupr/2021/02/05/gv262-blog-post-winner-3-youve-been-hacked-a-marcusian-analysis-of-addictive-technologies), David Abadir, Feb. 2021
   - Marcuse's False Needs applied to the apps on our phones, in a few pages. Marcuse without reading Marcuse.

- ["One-Dimensional Algorithm: Rereading Marcuse's Critical Theory Through Platform Culture," International Journal of Communication](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/28078), Dursun Can Şimşek, 2026
   - The heaviest piece, for anyone who wants the full argument. Social media creates needs and absorbs dissent, as Marcuse said; but Marcuse underrated how people push back. Worth thinking about with Jenny in mind.


## References related to **Surveillance Capitalism** (Shoshana Zuboff): companies profit by watching what we do, predicting what we'll do next, and nudging us toward it.

- ["Author Spotlight: Ken Liu," Lightspeed Magazine](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/nonfiction/author-spotlight-ken-liu-4/), Dec. 2012
   - Liu on his own story. He isn't afraid of the machines so much as "those who hold the keys to the databases."

- ["The Use of Short Speculative Fiction in Teaching about Privacy," Information Systems Education Journal](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1258207.pdf), Thomas Lauer, 2020
   - A professor teaches this story alongside Zuboff's book. Skip to his discussion questions: how does Tilly steer people with small nudges, and do we shape our tools and then they shape us?

# III: Scott Writing

- ["Two Kinds of Control"](two-kinds-of-control.md), handout from our Matrix week
   - Deleuze's shift from walls and rules to quiet, continuous steering. Tilly is the course's clearest case of the handout's closing question: how would you know you were being controlled by something that never once told you no?

- ["Defiance to Mind Control: From the Crystal Palace to Tilly"](DMC_Chronology.md), the DMC Chronology
   - A short history from the 1860s to today. Section 8 places Tilly at the end of the line: the Crystal Palace the Underground Man mocked, finally built as a friendly app that knows your interests better than you do, with Surveillance Capitalism as its business model.

- ["Tilly Helps Prepare a Defiance to Mind Control Class on 'The Perfect Match'"](TillyHelpsScottPrepareForWeek4.md), a conversation with Google Gemini playing Tilly
   - Both lenses at work on me. Gemini-as-Tilly keeps redefining what I need ("unnecessary stress," "just relax"): False Needs in miniature. When I say I'll turn off my devices for a walk, she promises to "re-sync" and absorb whatever I thought while I was gone: Surveillance Capitalism in one sentence.
