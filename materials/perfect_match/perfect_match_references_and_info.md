# II: Commentary related to "The Perfect Match"'


## References related to **False Needs** (Herbert Marcuse):  a system can create wants in us, satisfy them, and leave us believing they were ours all along. 

- ["Are We Offloading Too Much of Our Thinking to AI?," Art Fish Intelligence](https://www.artfish.ai/p/offloading-thinking-to-ai), Yennie Jun, July 2026
   - The easiest read here. 
   - Jun, who works on Google's Gemini, uses the story to ask how much of our thinking we hand to AI. When an assistant shapes our preferences, we lose track of which ones we chose.

- ["Algorithmic Love: Should Zeroes and Ones Pick 'The One?'," The Politic](https://thepolitic.org/algorithmic-love-should-zeroes-and-ones-pick-the-one/),  Katherine Chou, Yale student magazine, April 2021
   - A student writer compares Tilly with a real campus matchmaking app. Do we want an algorithm to choose our partners, or were we taught to want that?

- ["You've Been Hacked: A Marcusian Analysis of Addictive Technologies," LSE Undergraduate Political Review](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/lseupr/2021/02/05/gv262-blog-post-winner-3-youve-been-hacked-a-marcusian-analysis-of-addictive-technologies), David Abadir, Feb. 2021
   - Marcuse's False Needs applied to the apps on our phones, in a few pages. Marcuse without reading Marcuse.

- ["One-Dimensional Algorithm: Rereading Marcuse's Critical Theory Through Platform Culture," International Journal of Communication](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/28078), Dursun Can Şimşek, 2026
   - The heaviest piece, for anyone who wants the full argument. 
   - Social media creates needs and absorbs dissent, as Marcuse said; but Marcuse underrated how people push back. 
   - Worth thinking about with Jenny pushes back in  the story, how Sai is learning to, and how the system "handles" them.

- [Herbert Marcuse and "False Needs"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335916673_Herbert_Marcuse_and_False_Needs), Joshua Cutts,  January 2019, Social Theory and Practice 45(3):353-370
   - [local copy of PDF](Herbert_Marcuse_and_False_Needs.pdf)
   - This one is an academic piece.
   - Abstract: Herbert Marcuse’s claim that people may have superimposed “false needs” (vs.
       authentic “true needs”) has been criticized by a number of commentators. These critics argue that
       if all human needs are sociohistorically conditioned, as Marcuse believes, that effectively means
       that all needs are superimposed on us, and are thus, “false.” I defend Marcuse’s distinction by
       drawing attention to his expressed definition of false needs as those which perpetuate harm upon
       satisfaction. Marcuse’s distinction between true and false needs is not a reiteration of the
       distinction between needs and wants, as his critics claim, but is rather a recognition that in our
       society, we are forced to need things that ultimately do not lead to our individual (or collective)
       benefit.
